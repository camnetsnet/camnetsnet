Discrete Hodge theory provides a calculus for functions defined on graphs and simplicial complexes, which makes it a broadly applicable tool in the analysis of data from discrete complex systems. Moreover, the relationship between the Hodge decomposition and analogous decompositions of vector fields and stochastic processes leads to new methods for analysis of dynamics on graphs. In this talk, I will introduce Hodge theory on graphs and present two applications. In the first application, we develop a pipeline for representing SDEs as a flow on a simplicial complex reconstructed from observed stochastic trajectories. Using the Hodge decomposition, we decompose the process into reversible and irreversible components. We validate our approach with numerical experiments and apply it to data from healthy and pathological human heartbeats and flickering red-blood cell experiments. In the second application, we show that the Hodge decomposition can be used to identify vortices in coupled oscillators on a lattice. Moreover, our approach defines the vorticity of dynamics on complex, heterogeneous networks, where we use the XY model as a case study. We apply our approach to human neuroimaging data to show vortex-like patterns on the human connectome. Finally, I will discuss how expanding neuroimaging data in a Hodge-Laplacian eigen basis generalises previous approaches such as ‘connectome harmonics’ to the ‘edge’ level, where I will highlight the importance of an appropriate normalisation.

Dr Ramón Nartallo-Kaluarachchi is a research associate in the Department of Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics (DAMTP) at the University of Cambridge. He holds a PhD in Mathematics from the University of Oxford.
