Many complex systems have both cooperative and antagonistic interactions. For example, countries form alliances and wage wars, financial assets move together or in opposite directions, genes activate or inhibit each other, and users of online platforms endorse or reject each other. We can represent these systems as signed networks, in which each edge has a positive or a negative sign. However, most tools of network science assume non-negative weights. The common practices of discarding negative edges, taking absolute values, or treating signs as ordinary weights can thus lead to wrong or incomplete conclusions. In this talk, we first review where signed networks appear and which problems arise when we apply standard methods to them. Then we discuss three types of analysis that account for signs explicitly: measuring structural balance at different scales, detecting mesoscale structure, and constructing network embeddings. Throughout, we illustrate how incorporating the signs into the analysis reveals structural properties that conventional approaches can overlook.

Prof Fernando Diaz-Diaz is an assistant professor in the department of applied mathematics at the Carlos III University of Madrid (UC3M).
